Hello, Dear Reader!

It is yet again time for another bedtime story about my tech journey. As you could have seen from the title, I have made a questionable purchase of getting a new laptop! This had to happen due to the fact that my laptop battery decided to *spontaneously combust* on me when I was compiling a way too big of a project.

Due to that unfortunate series of events I had to embark on a treacherous journey of getting a new piece of silicon that would give me access to the funny pictures on the Internet. As a self-proclaimed software engineer and, most importantly, a *tech enthusiast*, [Lenovo T430](https://www.reddit.com/r/thinkpad/) was my wet dream. The only *minor* downside was the fact that it is almost 15 years old and didn't have enough computational power to even open Microsoft Paint™®. That's why I had to settle on the next best thing which was a [Framework laptop](https://frame.work).

If you haven't heard of the company before, Framework™® positions itself as a manufacturer of modern, highly repairable and customizable laptops. And I believe that it delivers on the promise tenfold because I experienced that claim with my own clumsy hands: after two weeks of use, the input cover on my laptop decided to *exit the chat* and stopped registering half of the keypresses.

I stumbled upon the root cause of my newly found problem by attempting to remove the input cover from the main board. Due to amazing engineering at Framework, the input cover's riser cable isn't easily removed from the mainboard to prevent possible disconnects during transportation. However, the solder joints between the mainboard and the input cover connector weren't as structurally sound, so when I was attempting to remove my input cover, the connector decided to remove itself from the mainboard too...

So, now I had a piece of tech that I couldn't even *turn on*, which I wasn't planning on having after getting a brand new laptop. Thankfully, the Framework team was really helpful and they replaced my mainboard free of charge, which only took me *40 minutes* to replace (which in non-tech terms is performing a heart surgery). So, the moral of the story is: get tech that lasts, so that there would be less e-waste in the world. Thank you for reading my rant and have a good day!
